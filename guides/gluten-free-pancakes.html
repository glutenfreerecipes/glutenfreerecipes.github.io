Gluten-Free Pancakes

Gluten-free pancakes are one of the easiest gluten-free breakfasts, and a good one is light, fluffy, and golden. The recipe is simple, the batter rests for a few minutes, and the pancakes cook in a couple of minutes each. The challenge is getting the texture right: too much flour makes them heavy, too little makes them fall apart, and a pan that is too hot burns them before they cook through. This guide gives a reliable recipe, explains the technique, and offers topping ideas for sweet and savoury pancakes.

A reliable pancake recipe

A good recipe uses 200 grams of gluten-free self-raising flour, a tablespoon of sugar, a pinch of salt, 2 eggs, 250 millilitres of milk, and a tablespoon of melted butter. Whisk the dry ingredients, then whisk in the eggs, milk, and butter until the batter is smooth. Let the batter rest for ten minutes, which gives the flour time to absorb liquid and improves the texture. Heat a lightly oiled pan over a medium heat, pour in a small ladle of batter, and cook for about two minutes until bubbles appear on the surface, then flip and cook for another minute. This makes about eight medium pancakes.

Getting fluffy pancakes

Fluffy pancakes need a batter that is thick enough to hold its shape but loose enough to spread a little. If the batter is too thick, add a splash of milk; if it is too thin, add a spoonful of flour. Do not over-mix the batter: stir until just combined, because over-mixing makes pancakes tough. Let the batter rest, which is the single most important step for gluten-free pancakes. Cook on a medium heat, not a high one, so the pancakes cook through without burning. Keep cooked pancakes warm in a low oven while you cook the rest.

Sweet topping ideas

Classic toppings are lemon and sugar, maple syrup, or berries with yoghurt. Banana and honey is a popular combination, and a spoonful of nut butter with sliced banana makes a filling breakfast. For a lighter option, serve pancakes with fresh fruit and a dollop of Greek yoghurt. Avoid very sweet toppings every day: a little maple syrup is fine, but a large amount of syrup and chocolate spread turns a healthy breakfast into a dessert. Fresh fruit is the best topping for flavour and nutrition.

Savoury pancakes and storing

Savoury pancakes leave out the sugar and add herbs, cheese, or vegetables to the batter. A spinach and cheese pancake, or a sweetcorn pancake, makes a good lunch. Cooked pancakes keep in the fridge for two days in a sealed container, and they freeze well: layer them with baking paper and freeze for up to a month, then reheat in a toaster or a dry pan. Leftover batter keeps in the fridge overnight, but stir it gently before using it, because it thickens as it rests.

Pancakes for the weekend

Pancakes are a weekend treat in many families, and gluten-free pancakes are no exception. Make the batter the night before, so the morning is relaxed, and cook the pancakes while everyone is at the table. Let each person choose their own toppings, such as fruit, yoghurt, or a little maple syrup, which makes breakfast fun for children. Cook a double batch and freeze the extras for busy weekday mornings, when they can be reheated in a toaster. A slow pancake breakfast is a good way to start the weekend, and it shows that gluten-free food can be a pleasure rather than a restriction.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are my gluten-free pancakes flat?

The batter may have been over-mixed, or the pan was not hot enough. Rest the batter for ten minutes and cook on a medium heat.

Can I make gluten-free pancake batter the night before?

Yes. Store it in the fridge overnight and stir it gently before cooking, as it will have thickened.

What flour is best for gluten-free pancakes?

A 1-to-1 gluten-free self-raising flour blend works best. Rice flour alone can make pancakes gritty.
