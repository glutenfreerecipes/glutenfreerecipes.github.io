Gluten-Free Holiday Recipes

Holidays and celebrations are built around food, and a gluten-free diet can make them feel stressful: the Christmas dinner, the birthday cake, the Easter lunch, and the summer barbecue all have wheat at their centre. But with a little planning, a gluten-free holiday can be just as enjoyable, and most guests will not notice the difference. This guide gives recipes and tips for the main celebrations, so you can enjoy the food without worrying about gluten.

Christmas dinner

A traditional Christmas dinner is mostly gluten-free already: roast turkey, roast potatoes, vegetables, and gravy made with cornflour instead of wheat flour are all safe. Check the stuffing, which is usually made with bread: make a gluten-free stuffing with gluten-free bread, or serve a rice-based stuffing instead. Check the pigs in blankets and the sausages, because some contain wheat rusk. Christmas pudding is often made with wheat flour, so make a gluten-free version with gluten-free breadcrumbs, or serve a flourless chocolate cake instead. Mince pies need gluten-free pastry, which is now widely available ready-made.

Birthday cake

A birthday cake is the centrepiece of a celebration, and a gluten-free one should look and taste just as good. A chocolate cake made with a 1-to-1 flour blend, or a flourless chocolate cake, works well and is easy to decorate. An almond cake is moist and keeps well. Make the cake the day before and store it in a sealed container, then decorate it on the day. If you buy a cake, order it from a gluten-free bakery and check that it is made in a gluten-free kitchen. Always label the cake clearly if it is part of a buffet with wheat cakes.

Easter and other celebrations

Easter is easier than it looks: most chocolate Easter eggs are gluten-free, but check the label, because some contain biscuit or wafer. Hot cross buns need a gluten-free recipe, but they are simple to make with a 1-to-1 flour blend and mixed fruit. For other celebrations, choose dishes that are naturally gluten-free: a roast dinner, a curry with rice, or a barbecue with grilled meat and salads. For a barbecue, cook gluten-free food first on a clean grill, and keep it separate from bread rolls and burgers with wheat buns.

Planning ahead

Planning is the key to a stress-free gluten-free holiday. Check recipes and labels in advance, and make a shopping list so you do not forget anything. Cook and freeze dishes ahead of time, so you are not cooking everything on the day. Tell your host in advance if you are going to someone else's celebration, and offer to bring a dish you can eat. Keep a few safe snacks with you, in case there is nothing suitable. Most importantly, enjoy the celebration: food is part of it, but it is not the whole of it.

Planning a gluten-free holiday

A gluten-free holiday is enjoyable with a little planning. Check recipes and labels in advance, make a shopping list, and cook what you can ahead of time. Tell your host what you need, and offer to bring a dish you can eat, so there is always something safe for you. Pack a few snacks for the journey, and keep a list of safe restaurants near your destination. Most importantly, focus on the people and the celebration, not just the food. With preparation, a gluten-free holiday can be just as joyful as any other, and the food can be just as good.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I eat Christmas pudding if I am gluten-free?

Only if it is made with gluten-free breadcrumbs and flour. Most shop-bought Christmas puddings contain wheat, so make your own or check the label.

Are chocolate Easter eggs gluten-free?

Most plain chocolate eggs are gluten-free, but check the label, because some contain biscuit, wafer, or cookie pieces.

How do I handle a barbecue if I am gluten-free?

Cook your food first on a clean grill, keep it separate from bread rolls, and check sauces and marinades for wheat.

Important: if you have celiac disease or a wheat allergy, always check labels and look for certified gluten-free products. This site is general information only — talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian before changing your diet.
