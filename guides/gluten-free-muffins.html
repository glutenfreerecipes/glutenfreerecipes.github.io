Gluten-Free Muffins

Gluten-free muffins are a forgiving bake and a useful one: they are quick, they freeze well, and they work for breakfast, lunchboxes, and snacks. A good gluten-free muffin should be light, moist, and domed on top, not dense or crumbly. The key is a wet batter, a short rest, and a hot oven. This guide gives a reliable base recipe, flavour ideas, and the fixes for the most common muffin problems.

A reliable base recipe

A good base recipe uses 250 grams of gluten-free self-raising flour, half a teaspoon of xanthan gum if your blend does not include it, 100 grams of sugar, 2 eggs, 100 millilitres of oil or melted butter, and 150 millilitres of milk. Mix the dry ingredients in one bowl and the wet ingredients in another, then combine them with a few strokes until just mixed. Let the batter rest for ten minutes, then spoon it into a lined muffin tin, filling each cup about two-thirds full. Bake at 200 degrees Celsius for 18 to 20 minutes, until risen and golden. Cool in the tin for five minutes, then move to a wire rack.

Getting a good rise

Muffins rise best in a hot oven, so preheat it fully before you start. Fill the cups two-thirds full, not more, because overfilled muffins spill and bake unevenly. Do not over-mix the batter: stir until the flour is just combined, because over-mixing makes muffins tough. Letting the batter rest for ten minutes helps the flour absorb liquid and improves the rise. If your muffins have flat tops, the oven may have been too cool, or the batter may have been over-mixed. A hot oven and a light hand are the secrets to a good dome.

Flavour ideas

Blueberry muffins add 150 grams of blueberries to the base recipe. Chocolate muffins add 30 grams of cocoa powder and 100 grams of chocolate chips. Banana muffins use two mashed bananas in place of half the milk. Apple and cinnamon muffins add a chopped apple and a teaspoon of cinnamon. Cheese muffins leave out the sugar and add 100 grams of grated cheese and a pinch of mustard powder, and they are good for lunchboxes. Lemon and poppy seed muffins add the zest of a lemon and a tablespoon of poppy seeds. Each variation starts from the same base, so you can try them all.

Storing muffins

Gluten-free muffins are best on the day they are baked, but they keep for two days in a sealed container at room temperature. For longer storage, freeze them in a sealed bag once cool, and defrost at room temperature or in the microwave for 30 seconds. They freeze well for up to three months. To revive a muffin that has gone slightly stale, warm it in the oven for five minutes. Avoid storing muffins in the fridge, because they dry out faster there. If you make muffins for lunchboxes, wrap them individually so they stay fresh.

Muffins for the week

A batch of muffins on Sunday gives you breakfasts and snacks for the whole week. Bake twelve muffins, let them cool, and freeze them in a bag, then defrost one or two each morning as needed. Try a different flavour each week, such as blueberry one week and banana the next, so you do not get bored. Muffins are easy to pack for work or school, and they are a good alternative to biscuits or cereal bars. A weekly muffin batch takes less than half an hour and makes busy mornings much easier.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are my gluten-free muffins dense?

Usually the batter was over-mixed, or the oven was not hot enough. Stir until just combined and preheat the oven fully.

Can I freeze gluten-free muffins?

Yes. Freeze them once cool in a sealed bag for up to three months, and defrost at room temperature.

Should I rest gluten-free muffin batter?

Yes, for ten minutes. It gives the flour time to absorb liquid, which improves texture and rise.
