Gluten-Free Dinner Recipes

Gluten-free dinners do not need to be different from family dinners. Most evening meals are naturally gluten-free or easy to adapt, because they are based on meat, fish, vegetables, rice, or potatoes rather than wheat. The trick is choosing dishes that everyone can eat, so you cook once instead of making a separate meal. This guide gives five easy gluten-free dinners that work for the whole family, with notes on adapting them and using leftovers.

Chicken stir-fry with rice noodles

Slice two chicken breasts and fry them in a little oil until cooked through. Add sliced peppers, broccoli, and carrots, and stir-fry for five minutes. Add a sauce made from tamari, which is gluten-free soy sauce, a teaspoon of honey, and a squeeze of lime. Serve with rice noodles, soaked in hot water until soft. This dish is quick, colourful, and naturally gluten-free as long as you use tamari instead of regular soy sauce. It also works with prawns, tofu, or beef, and any vegetables you have in the fridge.

Beef and vegetable curry with rice

Fry an onion, garlic, and ginger in oil, add two tablespoons of mild curry paste, and check that the paste is gluten-free. Add 500 grams of diced beef, a tin of chopped tomatoes, and a tin of coconut milk, and simmer for 45 minutes until the beef is tender. Add a handful of spinach at the end. Serve with basmati rice and a spoonful of yoghurt. Curry paste and stock cubes can contain wheat, so check the label every time. This curry freezes well, so make a double batch and save half for another night.

Gluten-free pasta with tomato and basil

Cook gluten-free pasta in plenty of boiling water, checking it a minute before the packet time so it does not go mushy. Meanwhile, fry garlic in olive oil, add a tin of chopped tomatoes and a pinch of salt, and simmer for ten minutes. Toss the pasta with the sauce, add fresh basil and grated parmesan, and serve with a green salad. Use a good quality gluten-free pasta made from rice or chickpea, and save a cup of the cooking water to help the sauce cling to the pasta. This is a fifteen-minute dinner that the whole family will eat.

Salmon with roast vegetables and potatoes

Place salmon fillets on a baking tray with chopped potatoes, carrots, and courgette, drizzle with olive oil, and roast at 200 degrees Celsius for 25 minutes until the salmon is cooked and the vegetables are golden. Season with salt, pepper, and lemon. This is a simple, healthy dinner with almost no washing up, because everything cooks on one tray. It is naturally gluten-free, high in omega-3 fats, and easy to scale up for guests. Serve with a green salad or steamed green beans.

Lentil and vegetable shepherd's pie

Fry an onion, carrots, and celery in oil, add 200 grams of red lentils, a tin of chopped tomatoes, and 500 millilitres of vegetable stock, and simmer for 25 minutes until thick. Check that the stock is gluten-free. Spoon the lentil mixture into a baking dish, top with mashed potato, and bake at 190 degrees Celsius for 25 minutes until golden. This is a filling, high-fibre dinner that costs very little and feeds a family. It freezes well in portions, so make extra for busy evenings.

Dinner for the whole family

The best gluten-free dinners are the ones the whole family eats together, without anyone feeling different. Choose dishes that are naturally gluten-free, such as curries, stir-fries, roast dinners, and pasta, so you cook once and everyone enjoys the same meal. Let family members choose their own toppings or sides, which gives them a sense of choice. Cook a double batch when you can, and freeze the extra for a busy evening. A family dinner, eaten together at the table, is one of the pleasures of home cooking, and gluten-free food fits into it easily.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the whole family eat gluten-free dinners?

Yes. Most gluten-free dinners, such as curries, stir-fries, and roast dinners, are meals everyone can enjoy, so you cook once.

Is soy sauce gluten-free?

Regular soy sauce contains wheat. Use tamari, which is usually gluten-free, and always check the label.

How do I check if a stock cube is gluten-free?

Read the ingredients and look for wheat, barley, or a gluten-free claim. Many stock cubes are gluten-free, but recipes change, so check every time.

Important: if you have celiac disease or a wheat allergy, always check labels and look for certified gluten-free products. This site is general information only — talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian before changing your diet.
